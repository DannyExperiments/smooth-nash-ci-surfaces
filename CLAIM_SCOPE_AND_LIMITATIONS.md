# Claim scope and limitations

The theorem applies to binary games on the disjoint union of two edges and any finite number of isolated vertices. The payoff space is the full complex affine space with a separate unrestricted array for each player. For every fixed graph of this form, there is a nonempty Zariski open subset of payoff arrays where the Nash CI variety is a smooth complex projective surface. In particular the dimension is two and the surface is nonempty. Prior work already establishes generic irreducibility.

The theorem leaves arbitrary partitions, restricted payoff subspaces, other action counts, special payoff tables, real positive equilibrium existence and topology, semialgebraic boundary classification, and the ED-degree question outside its scope. The diagnostic replay works over finite fields. The mathematical proof supplies the complex generic claim.

For a two-by-two edge matrix over any field, the independent payoff equations identify all three row-base lines. Their intersections with the column-base lines consist precisely of the four coordinate axes and the checkerboard line in the affine cone.
