# Q220: Generic Nash CI surfaces for two disjoint edges are smooth

**Theorem.** For a binary game whose graphical model is the disjoint union of two edges and r≥0 isolated vertices, the Nash conditional-independence variety for generic complex payoff arrays is a smooth projective surface. Generic irreducibility is already established by [Bouyer, Portakal and Sendra-Arranz, Theorem 4.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.11467v1#S4). Generic means on a nonempty Zariski open subset of the affine space of all payoff arrays.

This answers [pinned Q220](https://github.com/10m-u/open-problems-catalog/blob/ca8d4d44ba3db527f19f28928c240168f545ecfe/problems/game_theory/noncooperative-games-equilibrium-theory.md#q220), the surface question from Sendra-Arranz's thesis and the discussion after Corollary 28 in [Portakal–Sendra-Arranz, Game Theory of Undirected Graphical Models](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.13246#S5). The point is to prove smoothness across the complex boundary, not just off the union of the tensor-coordinate and total-mass hyperplanes.

## 1. The equations and their payoff parameter spaces

Write M=P³_A × P³_B × (P¹)^r. The variables A and B are 2×2 matrices of homogeneous coordinates for the two edges. The tensor is their Segre product with the singleton factors. This is the closure of the clique-factorized conditional-independence model (and agrees with it on the dense chart of nonzero tensor coordinates under discussion).

For a row player in A write R_i for the sum of row i (i=0,1). Let L_i be the sum of the products of the affected row's entries and the entries of the other factors, with coefficients given by the payoff entries for action i. The coefficients for the two actions are arbitrary and independent. Comparison of the two conditional payoffs on the open chart is equivalent, after cancelling the common marginal factors from other cliques, to
```
f = R_1 L_0 - R_0 L_1 = 0.
```
The column player instead uses column sums and column-supported payoff forms. The players of B have the same construction with A and B exchanged. For a singleton player the corresponding reduced equation is a general multilinear form on the other factors, independent of that player's own P¹ coordinate: the numerator and denominator each contain its action coordinate, so on the open chart these cancel and the remaining denominator is the same for both actions. This supplies n=r+4 equations on M of dimension n+2. Each player's equation uses a separate payoff parameter block. At a fixed choice of other projective factors, the coefficients of a paired player's L_i as a linear form on the corresponding row (or column) can range freely: pick a nonvanishing monomial in the other factors and vary its coefficients in that payoff block. A singleton form can have arbitrary evaluation at every point of M.

Let Y denote the zero scheme of the reduced equations on all M. Write T for the full affine payoff space and D for the closed union of the tensor-coordinate and total-mass hyperplanes pulled back to the model, which are excluded in the definition of the Nash CI variety. These are proper hypersurfaces: in Segre coordinates the pullbacks are nonzero products of coordinates or sums of factor coordinates. The original equations and the reduced equations agree off D. We will show that for generic t∈T, Y_t is smooth of dimension two and no component is contained in D. This will identify Y_t with the requested closure.

## 2. The base strata in one edge factor

The base locus of a player's equation is the set where its evaluation is zero for every payoff array. In P³_A the base locus for the row player is the union of three lines: Z_0 (row 0 zero), Z_1 (row 1 zero), and S (both row sums zero). In fact the evaluation coefficient on the row-0 payoff forms is R_1 times row 0, and that on row-1 forms is −R_0 times row 1, yielding exactly this union. The column loci are the transpose.

Take the following finite stratification, applying it separately to A and B. The complement of the six lines has codimension c=0 and no base equation b=0. Each line with all line intersections removed is a smooth codimension-2 stratum with b=1. The same-player line intersections are points of codimension 3 with b=1. The row–column intersections are points of codimension 3 with b=2: they consist of the four matrices with a single nonzero entry, and the checkerboard matrix [1,−1;−1,1]. Indeed intersections of the row-zero and column-zero lines yield the four coordinate points; simultaneous zero row and column sums give the checkerboard; a row-zero with both column sums zero, or the transpose, is impossible in projective space. At no row–column point do the two base lines for any single player also intersect. The product of these strata with the singleton factors is a smooth stratum of M; codimensions and numbers of base equations add.

At any base point, a base equation's differential is supported in the cotangent space of its own edge factor, since the equation vanishes there for *every* choice of other factors. It kills the tangent to the stratum. The following descriptions are as linear images of payoff parameters, using local representatives of the homogeneous coordinates.

- On the open portion of a row-zero line, say row 0 is zero, R_1 is nonzero. Then d f=R_1 d L_0 − L_1 d R_0, and varying L_0 gives every linear form in the two row-0 coordinate differentials. On the row-sums line away from intersections, both rows are nonzero and d f=L_0 dR_1−L_1 dR_0; the independent values L_0,L_1 give the full two-dimensional conormal. The same holds for column lines.
- At a same-player intersection, say row 0 and R_1 are zero, the differential is −L_1 dR_0. Its image has dimension one because row 1 is nonzero. The identities for the other intersections follow by transpose or switching actions.
- At a row–column coordinate point, e.g. A_00=1 and all other entries zero, the differential images are the two different two-dimensional spaces spanned by disappearing row coordinates and disappearing column coordinates respectively. At the checkerboard point the two images are the spans of row-sum and column-sum differentials respectively, also distinct two-planes: the four sum forms have one total-sum relation and joint rank three. In both kinds of points the images are independently attainable across the two payoff blocks.

## 3. Generic fibers are smooth on and across the strata

Fix a stratum S' of codimension c with b base equations. The locus of pairs (p,t)∈S'×T satisfying the n−b nonbase equations is a vector bundle over S' of rank dim(T)−(n−b). Indeed each nonbase evaluation is a nowhere-zero linear map from a different parameter block to its one-dimensional line bundle, so the combined evaluation is surjective and locally splits. Consequently that incidence space is smooth and has dimension dim(T)+2−c+b.

By generic smoothness in characteristic zero applied to its projection to T, a generic fiber is empty or smooth of dimension 2−c+b and the nonbase differentials restricted to T_p S' are independent. In detail, for a dominant projection from this smooth incidence space to smooth T, the locus of points where the differential of the projection is not onto has image in a proper Zariski closed subset of T, by generic smoothness over the target. For a nondominant projection exclude the closure of its image. Surjectivity of the differential of the projection is equivalent to surjectivity of the p-derivative of the nonbase equations on T_p S', since their payoff derivative is surjective. There are finitely many strata, so generic t may be chosen for all of them at once.

It remains to exclude dependence of base differentials in the normal directions. Different edge factors give independent cotangent summands. Within one factor, the bad set in the base-player payoff blocks, at fixed p, has the following codimensions:
- On a one-base open line, the base differential is zero in a two-dimensional image, a codimension-2 condition.
- At a same-player intersection point, zero in a one-dimensional image has codimension 1.
- At a two-base point the independent images are distinct two-planes in a three-dimensional space. Dependent ordered pairs in their product consist of the two zero-vector loci (each codimension 2), and pairs in the common one-dimensional intersection (also codimension 2).

The base-player parameters are independent of the nonbase equations in this incidence calculation. If a stratum has an open line or two-base point in some factor, c−b≥1, so its incidence dimension is at most dim(T)+1 and the codimension-2 bad subset has dimension at most dim(T)−1. If a stratum has a same-player intersection point, c−b≥2, so its incidence dimension is at most dim(T) and the corresponding codimension-1 condition also gives dimension at most dim(T)−1. Taking the union of the losses of base rank, and then the closures of their projections, removes only a proper closed subset of T. On the remaining generic fibers, the base differentials are independent and annihilate the stratum tangent, while the others restrict independently to it. Thus the full n differentials are independent at every point of Y_t. The Jacobian criterion gives smoothness and local dimension two everywhere.

## 4. Nonemptiness and taking the closure

For nonemptiness, the product of the n divisor classes in the Chow ring of M is nonzero. The four paired-player classes have positive coefficient on their own factor hyperplane (in fact two), and each singleton equation has coefficient one on each factor other than its own. For r=0, choose the monomial A²B² by picking each paired player's own class. For r=1, choose A³B² by picking A for the singleton. For r≥2, assign the singleton equations cyclically to the other singleton factors by a cyclic shift, obtaining A²B² times the product of all singleton classes. Each monomial survives in the Chow ring as all exponents are within the factor dimensions; there is no cancellation because every coefficient is nonnegative. A nowhere-zero section of the direct sum of the n equation line bundles would force its top Chern class to vanish, so their common zero set is nonempty.

Every stratum with a base equation has generic fiber dimension at most one. On the zero-base open stratum, restricting the incidence bundle to D gives total dimension at most dim(T)+1 since D has dimension at most dim(M)−1. By the fiber dimension theorem the generic fiber there also has dimension at most one, stratifying into irreducible locally closed components if necessary. Thus no two-dimensional component of Y_t is supported on D. The complement Y_t minus D is dense in every component, and the Segre embedding is closed. The saturated closure defining the Nash CI variety is Y_t, which is smooth and projective of dimension two. The cited Proposition 29 gives connectedness for generic games; a smooth connected complex variety is irreducible because its distinct irreducible components cannot meet.

This is an algebraic proof over complex parameters. Internally reviewed, not peer reviewed. The optional replay checks just the local base geometry; it is not a premise of the proof.
